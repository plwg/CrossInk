inki

Anki decks for your CrossInk reader

1Your Anki export

In Anki, choose File › Export, pick Anki Deck Package and tick Include scheduling information so your progress comes along. (An Anki Collection Package works too.)

2Study settings

Each deck file keeps its own settings, so the reader needs nothing else. These are used for the decks you download in step 3.

FSRS-7 has default parameters. Training on the review history in your export fits them to how you remember.

3Put a deck on the reader

Download a deck file and copy it into the flashcards folder on the SD card. Subdecks are included.

Choose your Anki export first.

4Update a deck on the reader

Take the deck's .inki file from the SD card's flashcards folder to add cards you added in Anki (with a fresh export in step 1) or to change its settings. Copy the downloaded file back over the old one. Progress made on the reader is kept; don't study on the reader in between.

5Chinese and Japanese cards once

The reader's built-in fonts show English and other Latin-script languages (pinyin tone marks too) and Cyrillic, but not Chinese characters or Japanese kana. Put this font on the SD card once and every deck can use it; your books keep their own font.

Download Noto Sans CJK SC (10 MB)
  1. Put the reader's SD card in your computer and open the downloaded zip.
  2. Copy the Noto Sans CJK SC folder (the whole folder, not the files inside it) into the fonts folder at the top of the SD card. Create fonts if it isn't there. The other two files in the zip stay on your computer.
  3. Check the card has fonts/Noto Sans CJK SC/Noto Sans CJK SC_14.cpfont, and the same with _12, _16 and _18.
  4. Put the card back and turn the reader on. Open Settings › Reader › Font Options › Flashcard Font and choose Noto Sans CJK SC.

Korean is not included. The font is Noto Sans CJK by Adobe and Google, under the SIL Open Font License; the zip includes the licence.
